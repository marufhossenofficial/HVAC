Hiring Someone When Your AC Dies Shouldn't Feel Like a Trap

"They know I'm desperate — and that changes how they treat me."

When the AC goes out in July, every company you call knows you're not shopping. You're cornered. And you've probably heard enough stories about emergency markups and unnecessary repairs to make you want to slow down, ask more questions, call more people. But slowing down in this heat isn't an option.

"The quote I get won't actually tell me anything."

"New unit starting at $4,800" doesn't tell you what size you need, what brand you're getting, or what's included in that number. Most quotes leave out installation details, thermostat replacements, and haul-away fees — and you only find out when the final invoice is higher than expected. By then, it's already done.

"Whatever gets installed, I'm stuck with it for 15 years."

An AC unit is a 15-year decision. If it's undersized, it runs constantly and still leaves rooms hot. If the ductwork isn't checked, your energy bill stays high no matter what brand you put in. You can't take back a poor install — and the company that did it is rarely the one answering your calls two years later when something goes wrong.

How We Answer Each of Those Three Fears

On being cornered by your own urgency:

You get a written, itemized estimate before any work begins. Parts, labor, equipment, haul-away, permits — all on the page. If something unexpected comes up during an install, you hear about it before we proceed. The number you approve is the number on your invoice. Same written price whether you call in March or on a Saturday in August. Your urgency doesn't change our rate.

On quotes that don't mean anything:

Our technician walks you through what failed, why it failed, and what your options are — in plain language, before anyone picks up a tool. If a repair makes more sense than a replacement, we'll tell you that even though a new unit pays us more. Our business runs on referrals, and nobody refers a company that talked them into something they didn't need.

On the 15-year commitment:

Sizing is calculated from your actual home — square footage, ceiling height, ductwork condition — not copied from whatever unit was there before. You'll see the calculation, not just the recommendation. Every install is tested under load before we leave, and you get full documentation for your warranty records.

Wondering If We'll Just Tell You to Replace It?

Here's the Actual Math We Use

Here's the rule we apply: if a repair costs less than roughly 40–50% of a full replacement and your system isn't already at the end of its expected life, we recommend the repair — even though it's the smaller invoice for us.

What that looks like in practice: a system with 6–8 years of life left, a $900 repair needed, a $6,000 replacement cost — that's a repair. We'll tell you that. A 14-year-old system needing $3,200 of work against a $6,500 replacement? That's a different conversation — and we'll walk through it with you before you make any decision.

You don't have to take our word for the rule. Ask the technician to show you the numbers on your specific system. We'll show you.

This is also why some of our reviews specifically mention a technician recommending the cheaper option. That's not a coincidence.
